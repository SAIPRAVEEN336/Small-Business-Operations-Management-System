# Back Office: pitch

## Problem statement

Home tutors bill their families monthly, but they track who has paid in a notebook, a chat thread or their memory. Invoices go out late, payments are missed, and nobody follows up until the money is weeks overdue. The tutor ends the month unsure what is coming in, has to chase awkwardly by message, and cannot plan for rent or a salary to an assistant. Back Office replaces the notebook with one place that shows who owes money, drafts the reminder, and forecasts the cash.

## Persona

Thandi, a home tutor with 15 to 25 students billing monthly. She runs the business alone from her phone and a laptop, with a few recurring costs (rent, a part-time assistant, a software subscription). She is good at teaching and bad at admin, and she hates the moment of asking a parent for money.

## Who pays, how much, and why

The tutor pays. We suggest **R150 to R300 per month**.

Justification: one late invoice for a single student is assumed to be about R800 to R1,500 of a tutor's monthly billing (an estimate to validate with real tutors). If the app prevents even one month's invoice from going unpaid for six weeks, it covers its cost many times over. Against that, R150 to R300 is roughly the price of two or three tutoring lessons a month, and well under what she loses in a single missed payment and the time spent chasing it.

## Why she switches from a notebook

- **The Today page** answers "what do I need to do right now?" in one screen: who owes money, how late they are, and what to say.
- **The automatic chase list** orders customers by how overdue they are and drafts a polite message for each one, so chasing takes a tap instead of a difficult message written from scratch.
- A notebook cannot tell her what her cash will look like in 30 to 90 days. The forecast and what-if scenarios can.

## Five-slide outline

1. **The problem:** monthly billing, late payments, and the awkward chase, shown with a single notebook page.
2. **The product:** Today page and who-owes-me dashboard, with a live demo of an invoice going from sent to paid.
3. **The chase:** the automatic chase list and a drafted reminder, logged against the invoice.
4. **The forecast:** the 30 to 90 day cash view, plus one what-if scenario ("what if Mosaic Interiors pays 3 weeks late?").
5. **The ask and the price:** R150 to R300 per month, a free trial, and the next three tutors we want to sign up.

## Short answers

**What does the owner do on a bad day with five late customers?**
She opens the Today page, which lists the five customers ordered by how overdue they are. For each one she opens the chase page, reviews the drafted reminder, sends it through WhatsApp or copies it into email, and logs the follow-up. The app records that the reminder went out, so she does not have to remember who she has already chased. The whole task takes a few minutes rather than an afternoon of worrying.

**What is the minimum data the owner enters?**
Per customer: a name and payment terms in days (the default is 14). Per invoice: the customer, an amount and an issue date. Per payment: an amount, a date and a method. Optionally a phone number, which lets the reminder open directly in WhatsApp. Recurring bills and an opening cash balance are needed only for the forecast.

**Who pays, and why would they switch?**
The tutor pays R150 to R300 per month. She switches because the notebook cannot remind her, cannot show her what is overdue at a glance, and cannot tell her what her cash will look like next month. Back Office does all three, and one recovered late invoice pays for a year of the subscription.
